About the project

Understand the air around you.

Explore air quality monitoring data, discover nearby PM2.5 sensors, and examine historical trends across locations. This project makes environmental data easier to explore and understand.

What is this dashboard?

This dashboard combines an interactive map, available outdoor monitoring sensors, and historical readings to help users explore air quality across different regions.

Search for a location, explore the map, and save cities to compare their available historical data.

Understanding PM2.5

PM2.5 refers to fine particulate matter with a diameter of 2.5 micrometers or smaller. These particles can come from sources such as vehicle emissions, combustion, smoke, and other activities.

Because these particles are small enough to enter deep into the lungs, PM2.5 is an important pollutant to monitor. Concentrations can vary with weather, emissions, geography, and time.

What readings tell us

Measurements provide a snapshot of particulate concentrations at a particular monitoring location and time.

Why location matters

Nearby sensors may report different values because local conditions and sources of pollution vary.

Learn more from the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency .

Where does the data come from?

The dashboard discovers available monitoring sensors for the region being explored and retrieves their recorded measurements. Data availability and historical coverage differ by sensor.

How the dashboard works

  1. Search for a city or navigate to a region on the map.
  2. Retrieve available outdoor PM2.5 sensor information for that area.
  3. Load readings and display them on the interactive map.
  4. Explore saved locations and their available historical trends.

Technology

The project uses HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the interface; Leaflet for interactive mapping; D3.js for data visualization; Python and Flask for the backend; and Tiger Data with PostgreSQL and TimescaleDB for time-series storage and queries.

Limitations and responsible use

  • Sensor availability varies by region, and some areas may have limited monitoring coverage.
  • Readings may be delayed, missing, or affected by sensor quality and local environmental conditions.
  • A sensor measures conditions near its location; its reading does not necessarily represent an entire city.
  • Historical charts reflect the data available to the dashboard, not necessarily a complete record for every day.

This dashboard is intended for exploration and education. It should not replace official public-health guidance or local air quality advisories.

For current public information, visit AirNow .
