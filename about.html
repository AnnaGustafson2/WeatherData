Your air, your health

What does your air quality mean?

Air pollution isn't always visible, but it can affect your health. The Air Quality Index (AQI) translates pollution measurements into six categories to help you understand when precautions are needed.

How healthy is your air?

Select an air quality level to learn what it means for your health.

Health effects become more likely for everyone, especially people with heart or lung conditions.

Reduce prolonged outdoor exertion. Sensitive groups should avoid prolonged or strenuous outdoor activity.

These categories use the U.S. Air Quality Index. AQI is not the same as a raw PM2.5 concentration measured in µg/m³. Recommendations are general; check local official guidance for current conditions. Learn more at AirNow ↗

Understanding PM2.5

PM2.5 refers to airborne particles 2.5 micrometers or smaller. These microscopic pollutants can come from vehicle exhaust, wildfires, industrial processes, and other combustion sources. Because they are so small, they can penetrate deep into your lungs.

02
📡

Where Data Comes From

Our dashboard uses air quality measurements from environmental monitoring sensors accessed through CMU CREATE Lab's ESDR platform. These sensors collect readings at different locations and times, helping us visualize local pollution patterns.

03
💻

Behind the Technology

Our application combines interactive maps, environmental data processing, and time-series analysis. Python and Flask handle the backend, Leaflet displays sensor locations, and TimescaleDB stores readings for historical exploration.

04
🔍

Understanding Limitations

Air pollution varies across neighborhoods, times, and weather conditions. Sensor coverage is uneven, and readings may be missing or delayed. A single measurement does not necessarily represent the conditions of an entire city.

05
🛡️

Using Data Responsibly

Air quality measurements are useful for identifying pollution trends and planning activities. However, readings should be interpreted alongside official advisories, particularly during wildfires and severe pollution events.
